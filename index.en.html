Project Overview

Purpose · Philosophy · Structure

A project that re-examines the artistic spirit, way of life and philosophy of Geomyeo Yu Hui-gang, reinterpreting and recreating them with a contemporary sensibility, to grow Geomyeo into an art brand representing Seohae-gu, Incheon.

This is not a single outcome but a new beginning that will continue onward.Kim Seong-ha, CEO, Incheon Seohae-gu Culture Foundation — from the catalog foreword
  1. Re-examining a master of modern Korean calligraphy

    Though a leading artist from Seohae-gu, Incheon, his work had been shown only in limited exhibitions by a few institutions; an integrated program embracing his entire artistic world was needed.

  2. The 50th anniversary — from commemoration to expansion

    Through a commemorative project spanning exhibition, competition and scholarship, we redefined the present-day meaning of Geomyeo's art.

  3. A contemporary reinterpretation of traditional art

    A multi-part program linking the calligraphy competition, exhibition and symposium demonstrated the contemporary potential of traditional calligraphy.

  4. Reaching the public through participatory cultural events

    A nationwide calligraphy competition, an exhibition of award-winning works and an awards ceremony created a cultural platform shared by artists, scholars and citizens.

  5. Laying the groundwork for a regional cultural brand

    Sustained promotion through local press and online media secured the potential of a flagship arts-and-culture brand for Seohae-gu, Incheon.

1.1Core philosophy — Perception · Reflection · Action

認知

Perception — Exhibition

To face a subject and come to know it. At the special exhibition, visitors encounter Geomyeo's works first-hand.

思惟

Reflection — Scholarship

To think deeply about meaning and interpret it. The symposium deepens understanding of his artistic world.

行爲

Action — Calligraphy

To practice and express directly, grounded in reflection. At the competition, participants carry on Geomyeo's spirit with the brush.

By expanding the enjoyment of art into the staged experience of perception, reflection and action, we turn it from passive viewing into active experience, laying the basis for the sustained growth of regional cultural assets and for glocal cultural branding.

Geomyeo Yu Hui-gang 1911–1976

Writing sharp as a blade, firm as stone,
and round as a gourd

Yu Hui-gangart name Geomyeo · clan seat Jinju · born in Sicheon-dong, Seohae-gu, Incheon

Born in 1911 in today's Sicheon-dong, Seohae-gu, Incheon, he learned classical Chinese studies and calligraphy within his family. After Myeongnyun Academy (today's Sungkyunkwan University) he studied epigraphy and Western painting in China for eight years; on his return he introduced the stele-based bixue of the Northern Wei style to Korea, opening a new current in modern Korean calligraphy.

A cerebral hemorrhage in 1968 paralyzed his right side, but he took up the brush again with his left hand and, for the eight years until his death, pioneered a new realm of 'left-handed calligraphy' (左手書). He led Incheon's art world as director of the Incheon Metropolitan City Museum, received the Minister of Education Award at the National Art Exhibition and the Seoul Metropolitan Culture Prize, and was posthumously awarded the Order of Cultural Merit.

Formative years 1911 – 1937

  • 1911age 1

    Born the only son in Sicheon-ri, Seokgot-myeon, Bupyeong-gun, Gyeonggi-do (today's Sicheon-dong, Seohae-gu, Incheon). He lost his father just 28 days after birth and grew up under the teaching of his eldest uncle.

  • 1914age 4

    Shows exceptional talent while studying classical Chinese learning and calligraphy.

  • 1934age 24

    Enters Myeongnyun Academy (today's Sungkyunkwan University).

Years in China 1938 – 1946

  • 1938age 28

    Studies in China. Over eight years in Beijing, Shanghai, Nanchang, Hankou and elsewhere he researches Chinese painting-calligraphy and epigraphy and works as a newspaper copy-editor.

  • 1943age 33

    Studies Western painting for two years at the Shanghai Free Western-Painting Institute.

  • 1945age 35

    Serves as secretary to the commander of the Korean Liberation Army's Shanghai detachment.

Incheon activity · peak of right-handed calligraphy 1946 – 1967

  • 1946age 36

    Returns to Korea and to his family home in Sicheon-dong.

  • 1949age 39

    Takes part in founding the Incheon Artists' Association.

  • 1953age 43

    Selected at the 2nd National Art Exhibition for both a Western painting, 〈Yeom〉, and a calligraphy work, 〈Old Poem〉.

  • 1954~

    Serves as art teacher at Haeseong Middle School in Incheon, director of the Incheon Metropolitan City Museum, and director of the Incheon Metropolitan City Library.

  • 1956age 46

    Wins the Minister of Education Award in calligraphy at the 5th National Art Exhibition (and again at the 6th the following year). A recommended artist of the National Art Exhibition from 1958.

  • 1959age 49

    Holds his first solo exhibition at the Eunseong Tearoom in Incheon.

  • 1962age 52

    Receives the Gyeonggi Province Culture Prize. Opens the Geomyeo Seowon studio in Gwanhun-dong, Seoul, to train students.

  • 1967age 57

    Publishes 'On Wandang' in the journal Sasanggye. Holds the 1st Geomyeo Seowon Exhibition.

Left-handed calligraphy 1968 – 1976

  • 1968age 58

    Collapses from a cerebral hemorrhage in September, paralyzing his right side. Through painstaking effort he takes up the brush with his left hand and pioneers a new realm of left-handed calligraphy.

  • 1969age 59

    Invited to the 18th National Art Exhibition, where he shows left-handed work; he continues as an invited artist through 1976.

  • 1972age 62

    Receives the Seoul Metropolitan Culture Prize in the arts category.

  • 1975age 65

    Holds the exhibition 〈Geomyeo Yu Hui-gang: Left-handed Calligraphy〉 at Shinsegae Gallery and publishes the first volume of his Calligraphy Collection.

  • 1976age 66

    Begins the monumental 34-leaf Gwanseo Akbu but leaves it unfinished. Dies on 18 October from a recurrence of cerebral hemorrhage.

After his death 1977 – 2026

  • 1979

    Posthumously awarded the Order of Cultural Merit.

  • 1983

    A memorial exhibition of his works is held by the National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art.

  • 2006

    Named a 'Representative Figure of Incheon Arts & Culture' by the Incheon Foundation for Arts & Culture; a 30th-anniversary exhibition and symposium are held.

  • 2019

    The donation exhibition 'Geommu' is held at Sungkyunkwan University Museum.

  • 2024

    The special exhibition 'Cutting the World with a Brush' is held at the Jemulpo Club, Incheon.

  • 2026

    The Incheon Seohae-gu Culture Foundation launches the 50th-anniversary K-Culture Project.

Paper 1

Repositioning Geomyeo Yu Hui-gang and inheriting his artistic spiritFocusing on his course of study and artistic activity

Park Jae-bok · Professor, Division of Global Convergence, Kyungdong University

Analyzing Geomyeo's calligraphy — which introduced Northern Wei stele study and led a new paradigm in modern Korean calligraphy — along four axes, the paper showed that Geomyeo, long classed purely as a stele-school figure, had also absorbed the model-copybook tradition, and re-evaluated his left-handed work not as a mere record of rehabilitation but as a new creative period in his final years.

Incheon Ilbo · 6 Apr 2026 · Culture / Interview

“From the local to the world — expanding calligraphy into content”

Programs conceived on an international scale, through exhibitions and more · What sets it apart from past events: scalability and potential · CEO: “A cooperative structure that includes the family is the key”

This year, marking the 50th anniversary of the death of Geomyeo Yu Hui-gang (1911–1976), the ‘Geomyeo Yu Hui-gang K-Culture Project’ — linking a special exhibition, a calligraphy competition and an academic symposium — unfolded in Incheon last month. The differently formatted programs interlocked as a single attempt to read Geomyeo anew.

Kim Seong-ha, CEO of the Incheon Seohae-gu Culture Foundation, which led the project, described it in an interview with Incheon Ilbo on the 2nd as “a starting point for expanding regional cultural assets into content.”

Kim named ‘convergence’ as the starting point of the plan. “When Geomyeo’s life, works and artistic world operate together, they become powerful content,” he said. “I saw the potential to grow it into a cultural asset that represents not just Incheon but all of Korea.”

Geomyeo Yu Hui-gang was born in Sicheon-dong, Seokgot-myeon, Bupyeong-gun, Gyeonggi-do (today’s Sicheon-dong, Seohae-gu, Incheon) and is regarded as a master who carried on the calligraphic tradition after Chusa Kim Jeong-hui.

Even after a cerebral hemorrhage in 1968, he took up the brush with his left hand and pioneered ‘left-handed calligraphy.’

Kim also drew attention to the experimental, contemporary nature of Geomyeo’s work — a mode of expression that crosses the boundary between writing and painting and resonates with art today. “Art that breaks the mold is ultimately connected to how we see society,” he said, “and such an experience can be an occasion to expand an individual’s thinking.”

The structure of the project was designed on this understanding. The exhibition, competition and symposium were arranged not as a mere list but as a flow of ‘perception, reflection and action’: encountering the works through the exhibition, expanding interpretation through scholarly discussion, and taking part directly through the competition. “Art,” Kim explained, “is ultimately completed through the process of perceiving, thinking and acting.”

What sets this apart from past commemorations lies in its ‘scalability and potential.’

“Calligraphy can be turned not into a mere traditional art but into contemporary content,” he stressed, “a resource that can expand into new culture by combining with diverse genres such as webtoons, film, performance and public design.”

He also noted that, in the digital age, the very act of writing by hand can be an experience that restores human thought and sensibility.

Its meaning as a regional cultural asset is clear as well. “This is a process of repositioning Geomyeo not as a figure in memory but as a cultural asset of the city,” Kim said, “and content that begins in a region can become a ‘glocal’ model that expands to the world.”

His plans ahead are bigger still. He envisions expanding the exhibition, the academic conference and the competition to an international scale, and creating a ‘Month of Geomyeo’ in which programs run simultaneously in cities around the world. “This is not something the Seohae-gu Culture Foundation can do alone,” Kim said. “A cooperative structure in which the bereaved family, the art world and many institutions take part together is the key.”

Finally, he emphasized: “This commemorative project is not an end but a beginning. If we accumulate it over time, Geomyeo will become a cultural brand that expands beyond the region to the world.”

By Jeong Hoe-jin, Incheon Ilbo · hijung@incheonilbo.com

10

Roadmap

Beyond a one-off commemoration
toward a long-accumulating cultural brand

10.1Direction

  1. 1Now – short term

    Beginning in Seohae-gu

    In Seohae-gu, Incheon, we begin running ‘Geomyeo Month’ every April.

  2. 2Mid term

    From Incheon to the nation

    We expand ‘Geomyeo Month’ across all of Incheon and on to major cities nationwide.

  3. 3Long term

    To the world

    We spread ‘Geomyeo Month’ into an event joined by cities nationwide and abroad.

April

Geomyeo Month

Designating every April as ‘Geomyeo Month’, we concentrate Geomyeo content — exhibitions, scholarship, calligraphy — into a single month. Held at the same time each year, the exhibition, symposium and competition accumulate into the central axis of a long-building cultural brand.

  1. 01

    Growing a flagship arts & culture brand

    We grow Geomyeo Yu Hui-gang into an arts-and-culture brand representing Seohae-gu, Incheon and Korea.

  2. 02

    A long-accumulating, cooperative project

    We pursue it not as a one-off commemoration but as a cooperation-based, long-accumulating project.

  3. 03

    A bottom-up glocal model

    We pursue a bottom-up glocal model that expands regional cultural assets into a K-Culture brand.

10.2Content expansion strategy

  1. SOURCESource IPGeomyeo's life, works & art
  2. STEP 1Tier-1 contentBuilding the base
  3. STEP 2Tier-2 contentGiving it form
  4. STEP 3Tier-3 contentIndustrialization
  5. GLOBALGlocal spreadAt home & abroad
Tier-1 content

Building the base

Exhibition
Developing into experiential exhibitions of perception-reflection-action, with replica shows alongside. Seohae-gu → Incheon → major domestic cities → overseas touring
Conference
Deepening themes year by year and building the findings into a database; shifting from a domestic conference to an international one
Competition
Moving away from mere copying toward a space of reflection and action that embodies Geomyeo's spirit; archiving winners by year and expanding into an international competition
Public design
Applying the Geomyeo typeface and work motifs to Seohae-gu's urban public design, turning them into regional brand assets
Tier-2 content

Giving it form

Typeface development
Designating it as the Foundation's official typeface and then spreading it to Incheon public bodies (with prior agreement on copyright and scope with the family and related institutions)
Calligraphy & left-hand writing
Developing experiential content imbued with Geomyeo's boundary-crossing spirit of experiment, and disseminating it through domestic and overseas cultural events and Korean Cultural Centers
Tier-3 content

Industrialization (examples)

Webtoon & web novel
Storytelling of Geomyeo's life and anecdotes
Film & documentary
A dramatic narrative such as pioneering left-handed calligraphy; entries to international film festivals
Musical & performance
Staging his artistic world; domestic premiere → overseas performance
Games
Licensing based on the Geomyeo universe
YouTube & SNS
Digital spread; running global channels
